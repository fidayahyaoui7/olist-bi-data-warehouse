# Business Needs Statement: Decision-Support System for Olist

## 1. Context

Olist, a Brazilian online marketplace, wants to improve its performance with a decision-support system.

The detailed context and the description of the nine source tables are in the project README.

## 2. Questions to Answer

These are the questions that were identified and that the system should be able to answer.

### 2.1 Sales and products

- Which products and categories sell best?
- Which products are losing momentum (revenue falling from one quarter to the next)?
- Which products contribute very little revenue for a given state and year? Could they be removed from the catalog?
- How do monthly revenue and the number of orders evolve? Is there seasonality (Black Friday)?
- Which states generate the most revenue?
- What share of the price paid is shipping cost, by product category?
- Does shipping cost depend on the weight and dimensions of the product?

### 2.2 Delivery and satisfaction

- What is the on-time delivery rate, by state and by month?
- What is the average delay of late orders?
- Do delivery delays lower the satisfaction score?
- Which product categories receive the lowest scores?

### 2.3 Customers

- Which customer groups can be identified (RFM segmentation: recency, frequency, monetary value)?
- Who are the highest-value customers (cumulative revenue)?
- What is the repeat purchase rate, and how does loyalty evolve by first-purchase cohort?
- Do satisfied customers (good score) buy again more often?

### 2.4 Sellers

- Which sellers make the most sales?
- Which sellers perform best by state, by category and by length of activity?
- Which sellers offer products that generate the most revenue, with the best scores and the fewest delays?

### 2.5 Payments

- Which payment methods are used most?
- Does paying in several installments increase the basket value?
- What is the average order value (basket) by category, by state and by payment method?

### 2.6 Statistical and advanced analyses

- What are the mean and standard deviation of revenue per order, per seller and per month?
- Which variables best explain the amount of an order (category, weight, customer state, number of installments, shipping cost)?
- Is there a significant difference between the mean revenue per seller in the state of São Paulo and that of sellers in other states?
- Is there a significant difference between the average score of orders delivered on time and that of orders delivered late?

## 3. Adapting the Needs to the Available Data

Some questions that are classic in a decision-support system (margin, discounts, sales representatives) cannot be asked as they are, because the Olist dataset does not contain the required information. They were reformulated rather than dropped.

| Classic question | Equivalent retained for Olist | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Margin generated by a product group | Revenue excluding shipping cost, and the share of shipping | No purchase cost data: we measure revenue, not margin |
| Do discounts increase sales or margin? | Does paying in several installments increase the basket? | No discount data in the tables |
| Sales representatives by country, gender, age, salary | Sellers by state, category, length of activity | Sellers are independent businesses, with no personal attributes |
| Country | Brazilian state | All orders are placed in Brazil |
| Most profitable customers | Highest-value customers | Same reason as for margin |
| Profitable suppliers | High-performing sellers (revenue, scores, delays) | Olist does not distinguish suppliers |

These limits are documented to avoid presenting indicators that would look right but rely on no data.

## 4. Functional Requirements

| Need | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Queries** | Query a measure along several axes, aggregate (roll-up) and drill down: year, quarter, month, day; state, city; category, product |
| **Reporting** | Four dashboards: management view (sales), delivery and satisfaction, customer analysis, seller performance |
| **Advanced analysis** | RFM segmentation, cohort analysis, regression, hypothesis tests (comparison of means) |

## 5. Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

| KPI | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue | Sum of item prices, excluding shipping cost, for orders that are not canceled and not unavailable |
| Number of orders | Number of distinct orders |
| Average order value | Revenue divided by the number of orders counted in revenue |
| Repeat purchase rate | Customers with at least two orders, divided by the total number of customers (identified by `customer_unique_id`) |
| On-time delivery rate | Orders delivered on or before the estimated date, divided by delivered orders |
| Average delay | Difference in days between the delivery date and the estimated date, for late orders |
| Average score | Mean of the satisfaction scores (1 to 5) |
| Cancellation rate | Canceled orders divided by all orders |

## 6. Analysis Axes and Measures

The questions above work at two different levels of detail, which leads to two fact tables that share three conformed dimensions (date, customer, order status): a fact constellation model.

| Domain | Fact table (grain) | Measures | Analysis axes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sales and products | `fact_sales` (one item of an order) | price, shipping cost | date, customer, product, seller, status |
| Delivery, satisfaction, payments | `fact_orders` (one order) | delivery time and delay, on-time delivery, amount paid, number of installments, payment method, satisfaction score | date (purchase, approval, shipping, delivery, estimated date), customer, status |

Every question in section 2 can be answered with these two tables and their five dimensions (date, customer, product, seller, order status).

## 7. Scope and Constraints

- **Period covered:** September 2016 to October 2018. Trend analyses cover January 2017 to August 2018 (20 complete months), because the first and last periods contain very few orders and November 2016 is missing.
- **Data quality:** 23 anomalies were identified and documented, with a handling rule for each one (duplicated geolocation rows, customer identifier generated per order, missing delivery dates, etc.).
- **Out of scope:** real-time data, purchase costs and margins, marketing campaigns.

## 8. Conclusion

We therefore need to build a data warehouse (a fact constellation model: two fact tables sharing conformed dimensions for date, customer and order status) able to answer the query, reporting and advanced analysis needs expressed above, fed by an ETL process that applies the documented data quality rules.
